Architects of Ephemeral Wonder

Founded under the curatorial vision of Falalu Creative Studio, Maison Événement emerged from an uncompromising conviction: that singular milestones demand spatial choreography as thoughtful, permanent, and tactile as haute couture or museum installations.

A confluence of
architectural rigor & sensory theatre.

"We construct transitory worlds that linger forever in collective memory—spaces where stone, silk, and fragrance converge."

Falalu Creative Studio Est. Place Vendôme, 2014

Our founders cut their teeth inside the hallowed workshops of classical French architecture, Parisian runway scenography, and the private hospitality wings of European nobility. Where conventional event production relies on prefabricated rentals and formulaic table settings, we treat every landscape as an unwritten spatial essay.

From hand-cast bronze candelabras and custom botanical distillations to transient structures drafted in CAD and fabricated by ancestral Tuscan joiners, our craft honors the raw romance of pure materials.

Today, Maison operates as an intimate multidisciplinary laboratory. We sit at the delicate intersection of historical restoration, sensory direction, culinary dramaturgy, and logistical mastery—quietly delivering landmark celebrations across secluded Italian lakes, historic French châteaux, and private island archipelagos.

Twelve Commissions.
Zero Compromise.

True bespoke architecture cannot be manufactured on an assembly line. To safeguard uncompromising depth of research, artisanal custom fabrication, and around-the-clock principal stewardship, Maison Événement limits its practice to strictly twelve commissions across each calendar year.

01

Unfettered Principal Immersion

Every commission is personally steered by Hélène Falalu and Julian Vance from initial schematic sketches through dawn teardown.

02

Prototype Fabrication

We build physical 1:1 scale mockups of bespoke lighting, tablescapes, and spatial partitions in our Paris workshop for private client review.

03

Discreet Concierge Autonomy

Absolute discretion, stringent privacy protocols, and dedicated security coordination for high-profile patrons and world dignitaries.

Three Pillars of Haute Scenography

Our methodology treats fleeting moments with the dignity and permanence of timeless monumental architecture.

Pillar I draw

Artistic Intention

We never replicate. Every celebration originates from a blank linen canvas, drawing inspiration from site archaeology, classical painting compositions, modern sculpture, and our patron's private heritage.

Site-Specific Geometry • Narrative Dramaturgy
Pillar II visibility_off

Logistical Invisibility

True luxury is effortless to experience yet titanic in execution. Our technical orchestrations—sound design, acoustic baffling, climate stabilization, and service flow—remain completely imperceptible to guests.

Acoustic Cloaking • Swiss-Trained Engineering
Pillar III interests

Bespoke Materiality

Sensory authenticity dictates our material palette: hand-loomed flax linens, hand-thrown porcelain tableware, reclaimed timber banquet tables, and living botanical perfumes designed for specific evening hours.

Raw Tactility • Olfactory Scenography

The Minds Behind the Architecture

A trinity of disciplines: pure spatial architecture, high-stakes international logistics, and living floral sculpture.

Founder • Paris

Hélène Falalu

Architectural Scenography & Direction

Trained in structural architecture at the École Nationale Supérieure d'Architecture in Paris and Milan’s Politecnico, Hélène spent a decade orchestrating monumental runway sets and couture showcases before founding Maison.

Operations • London

Julian Vance

Logistical Engineering & Operations

With a background leading operations for legendary palace hotels across Saint-Moritz, Cap d'Antibes, and Mayfair, Julian directs the complex logistical choreography—from private aviation logistics to 200-person culinary teams.

Botanics • Florence

Astrid Lindqvist

Head of Botanical Scenography

Celebrated across Scandinavian galleries and Venetian palazzi for her avant-garde botanical forms, Astrid rejects artificial foams in favor of historical Japanese ikebana mechanics, weaving native flora into grand living architectural sculptures.
